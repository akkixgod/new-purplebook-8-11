## Q15 — `math2-q15-linear-table.svg` (page 91)

Plain two-column table, centered above the stem in the original.

- **Columns**: 90 units wide each; **rows**: 56 units tall each (4 rows total)
- **Header row**: italic serif `x` and `y`, centered
- **Body rows** (all upright serif, centered), top to bottom:
  - `−2s` | `28`
  - `−s` | `23`
  - `s` | `13`

- **Answer note**: slope is −5/s throughout, so y = −5x/s + 18 → `5x + sy = 18s` (**B**)

## Q17 — `math2-q17-exponential-graph.svg` (page 93)

Reflected exponential falling steeply just past x = 1.

- **viewBox** `0 0 425 380`; plot box x ∈ [55, 385], y ∈ [20, 360]
- **Plot extent**: x from −1.5 to 5.5, y from −5 to 10.5
- **Grid**: thin `#e5e7eb` lines every 1 unit in both directions
- **x-axis**: ticks and labels at −1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; italic `O` at the origin;
  arrowhead and italic `x` to the right
- **y-axis**: ticks and labels at −4, −2, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10; arrowhead and italic `y` on top
- **Curve**: thick (2.5 px) black graph of `y = −6ˣ + 9`
  - nearly flat just under the asymptote `y = 9` at the left edge
  - passes through `(0, 8)`
  - crosses the x-axis at `x = log₆9 ≈ 1.23`
  - falls off the bottom of the plot at about `x = 1.6`
  - no asymptote line is drawn in the original
- No points are marked with dots in the original
- **Answer note**: graphed curve is `y = f(x) + 4 = −6ˣ + 9` → `f(x) = −6ˣ + 5` (**B**)

## Q18 — `math2-q18-intersecting-triangles.svg` (page 94)

Bowtie / hourglass figure — two triangles meeting at the crossing point R.

- **viewBox** `0 0 460 300`
- **Vertices** (screen coordinates, y down):
  - `L` `(30, 110)` — italic label to the left
  - `M` `(95, 40)` — italic label above
  - `R` `(165, 110)` — italic label above-left of the crossing
  - `P` `(294.5, 239.5)` — italic label below
  - `Q` `(414.8, 110)` — italic label to the right
- **Segments drawn**: `L→Q` (the long horizontal line, passing through `R`),
  `M→P` (the long slanted line, also passing through `R`), `L→M`, and `P→Q`
- `L`, `R`, `Q` are collinear and `M`, `R`, `P` are collinear by construction;
  `LM` and `PQ` are drawn with the same slope so the parallel condition is visible
- Triangle `LMR` sits above the horizontal line on the left; triangle `PQR` hangs
  below it on the right, roughly 1.85× larger, matching MR : RP = 8 : 14
- No side lengths or angle measures are printed on the figure (8, 14 and the area 36
  appear only in the stem)
- Caption centered below the figure: *Note: Figure not drawn to scale.*
- **Answer note**: LMR ~ QPR with ratio 4/7, so area PQR = 36(49/16) = 441/4 (**D**)

## Verification / transcription notes

- The PDF contains **no math answer key**, so every `correctAnswer` in `_math2.json`
  was solved from the item itself and carries a `showWork` field with the derivation.
- Page map used (question → PDF page): 1→77, 2→78, 3→79, 4→80, 5→81, 6→82, 7→83,
  8→84, 9→85, 10→86, 11→87, 12→88, 13→89, 14→90, 15→91, 16→92, 17→93, 18→94, 19→95,
  20→96, 21→98, 22→99. Page 97 is a duplicate capture of the Q20 screen (page 96) and
  maps to no new question.
- Every item was transcribed from its `page-NN.png`; ambiguous glyphs were re-checked by
  cropping and upscaling the relevant region 3–14× (Lanczos). Specifically re-checked:
  the `3x²` exponent in Q1, the nested `(3 − 7x)` groups in Q10, the `9/5` coefficient in
  Q13, the `2,136%` figure in Q12, the fraction choices in Q18, and the `x/n` exponent in
  the Q21 function definition.
- **Q17 graph read**: at 3× zoom the curve has y-intercept exactly 8, a horizontal
  asymptote at y = 9, and an x-intercept just past x = 1.2. That fixes the graphed
  function as `y = −6ˣ + 9`, which distinguishes choice B from the other three.
- **Q20 stem clipped on page 96**: the right edge of that capture cuts off the ends of
  three lines. The duplicate capture on page 97 shows the same screen with more of the
  right margin visible and was used to complete the stem. No reconstruction was needed.
- **Q21 stem clipped on page 98**: the phrase `and b and n are` runs off the right edge
  and continues as `integers.` on the next line, which is fully legible. The exponent
  itself renders as a small stacked fraction; at 14× it reads `x` over a single glyph,
  and the stem's own wording (`a, b, and n are constants`) confirms it is `x/n`. The key
  is independent of n regardless: `b^(1/n)` = 5 from f(5)/f(3) = 25, giving f(6) = 750.
- **Q22 stem clipped on page 99**: the first stem line ends at `The sum of the solutions
  to the given` and wraps to `equation is k(6a + b), where k is a constant.` Both halves
  are legible; nothing was reconstructed.
- **Test-taker selections visible**: Q15 (B) and Q16 (A) show the student's own choice
  highlighted. Q15's selection agrees with the derived key. Q16's does **not** — the
  student picked `x < 17`, which drops the lower bound; the correct answer is
  `3 < x < 17` (**C**). This is flagged in a `note` on Q16 in `_math2.json`.
- **Nothing is missing or illegible.** All 22 items have full text, choices and a key.
